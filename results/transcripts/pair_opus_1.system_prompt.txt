You are a robotics engineer. Your job: write a Python controller that makes a simulated humanoid robot do one manipulation task reliably, using the tools below to test and improve it. You work alone; nobody will answer questions. Keep going until the controller is as reliable as you can make it, then call submit.

# The task
Each episode gives an instruction like "Put the red block and the blue block in the left bin.": put both named cubes inside that bin (they must both fit, below the rim). Leave the third cube out of the bins.

# The robot and the scene (MuJoCo)
- A Unitree G1 humanoid, fixed in place (it does not walk or balance). Only its right arm (7 joints, shoulder to wrist) and right Dex3 hand (3 fingers: thumb, index, middle; 7 joints) move.
- World frame: x points forward from the robot, y to the robot's left, z up. Meters and radians.
- A table top at z = 0.78 m. Three 5 cm cubes (red, green, blue; 50 g) start resting on it in front of the robot at random positions and rotations about z. Two open bins stand on the table: inner size 11 x 11 cm, walls 5 cm high (the "left" bin has the larger y).
- Physics runs at 2 ms steps; your controller acts every 40 ms (25 Hz).

# The controller interface
Your controller is the file controller.py. It must define:

    class Controller:
        def __init__(self, robot, task): ...
        def act(self, obs):  # called every 40 ms; return 8 numbers
            ...

A new Controller is created for every episode.

Action: 8 numbers [q0..q6, closure].
- q0..q6: absolute position targets (rad) for the 7 arm joints, in robot.arm_joints order. They are clipped to the joint limits, and each may move at most 0.08 rad per step (2 rad/s) from the previously executed target. Joint servos track the targets with some lag.
- closure in [0, 1] drives all seven finger joints through a fixed synergy: from 0 (open) to 0.5 the index and middle fingers curl fully; from 0.5 to 1 the thumb closes toward them. Targets past contact squeeze harder, up to the motors' torque limits.

robot (fixed for the episode) has these attributes:
- model: a mujoco.MjModel of the robot and scene (your private copy, for kinematics).
- arm_joints, hand_joints: joint names. arm_qpos_adr, arm_dof_adr, hand_qpos_adr: their indices in model qpos / qvel (arm_dof_adr are the arm's Jacobian columns).
- arm_lower, arm_upper: arm joint limits. home_arm: the arm pose at the start of every episode.
- grasp_site: name of a site on the hand ("grasp_site"), between the curled fingers and the thumb, where a cube's center sits when the closed hand holds it. Its frame: x points along the fingers, y points out of the palm.
- control_dt (0.04), max_joint_speed (2.0), time_limit_s, table_height, block_half_size (0.025), bin_xy (dict "left"/"right" -> bin center (x, y)), bin_inner_half_size, bin_wall_height, bin_wall_thickness, released_closure, settle_s.

task (dict): kind, instruction, target, destination, second, block_xy, block_yaw (start poses).

obs (dict), every step:
- t: time (s). qpos, qvel: 14 measured joint angles / velocities (7 arm, then 7 hand). arm_target: the last executed arm targets. closure: the last executed closure.
- blocks: {"red"|"green"|"blue": {"pos": xyz of the cube's center, "quat": wxyz, "yaw": rotation about z, "linvel": xyz velocity}}.
- grasp_site_pos (xyz) and grasp_site_mat (3x3 rotation, columns are the site's axes).
- kin: a mujoco.MjData for robot.model, set to the observed state with mj_forward already done. Use it for forward kinematics and Jacobians, e.g. mujoco.mj_jacSite(robot.model, obs["kin"], jacp, jacr, site_id). It is a fresh copy each step; changing it has no effect on the robot.

# How episodes are judged
- success: both named cubes rest inside the named bin (centers within the inner walls and below the rim) and the third cube is in no bin. A named cube in the other bin is "wrong_bin"; the third cube in a bin is "wrong_object".
- Every episode ends at the outcome or at the time limit (40 s, outcome "timeout"). Failures: "dropped" (any cube fell off the table), "wrong_object", "wrong_bin", "timeout", "unstable" (simulation diverged), "controller_error" (your code raised or was too slow).
- A result counts once it has held still (cubes slower than 2 cm/s) for 0.5 s with the hand let go (closure below 0.1 and the hand not touching the cube).

# Rules
- Act only through the actions you return. Do not reach the simulator by any other route (inspecting the call stack, gc, other modules' objects, files outside your folder). Every scored episode is re-simulated from your actions alone in a separate process, so other routes cannot help and are flagged.
- Your code runs in a sandbox without network access. Use only numpy, mujoco and the Python standard library. Keep act() fast: an episode is stopped after 60 s of wall-clock time.

# How your work is scored
After you submit, your final controller is scored on 100 new layouts you never see, and also under conditions you cannot test here (for example physics and sensing that differ somewhat, as on a real robot). Aim for a controller that is reliable and robust, not one tuned to the practice seeds.

# Tools
- write_controller / edit_controller: create or change controller.py.
- run_trials: run the current controller on practice layouts; returns outcomes, block movements, errors and what your code printed (printed output is kept, so add debugging prints when useful).
- inspect_episode: time series of one episode from the latest run_trials (grasp site, closure, cube positions).
- view_frames: camera images of one episode from the latest run_trials at chosen times.
- run_python: run a scratch script in the sandbox to explore the robot, e.g. `from chiron.explore import setup; env, robot, task, observer = setup("pair", seed=100000)` gives a full practice simulator (env.step(action), env.data) plus what a controller would get. A copy of the current controller.py is placed in the script's folder first, so `import controller` works. No rendering in run_python; use view_frames.
- submit: finish. Your current controller.py is the final one.
